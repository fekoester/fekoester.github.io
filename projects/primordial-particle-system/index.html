Primordial Particle System

A fast C++ implementation of the Primordial Particle System (Schmickl et al., 2016): cell-like structures that grow and divide emerge from a one-line motion law.

Each particle turns by a fixed angle plus a term proportional to how many neighbours it has, signed by which side they are on. That is all, and it produces spores, cells and division.

Header-only core, SFML viewer, headless runs at roughly 2.3 million particle-steps per second.

Runs live in your browser

Watch cells emerge from one motion law

Every particle counts its neighbours within radius 5 on its left (L) and right (R), turns right by α + β·N·sign(R − L), and steps forward. Nothing else: no forces, no rules about cells. Give it a few hundred steps; nucleation is a rare event, so if nothing grows, press New world.

Parameters of Schmickl et al. (2016): r = 5, v = 0.67, α = 180°, β = 17°, on a torus. Particles here are updated simultaneously; the C++ version also offers the paper’s one-after-another update.
