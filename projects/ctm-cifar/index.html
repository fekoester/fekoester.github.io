Continuous Thought Machines on CIFAR

A reimplementation of Sakana AI’s Continuous Thought Machine for CIFAR-10/100 with a z-residual, normalised synchronisation, four tick losses, adaptive halting and a CNN baseline.

Continuous Thought Machines use neuron-level temporal dynamics and the synchronisation between neurons as the representation. This repo rebuilds the model for CIFAR and tests several design choices: tick-wise losses, evaluation rules, and adaptive halting.

Illustration, runs in your browser

Synchronisation as a representation

A Continuous Thought Machine does not read out neuron activations directly. It reads how pairs of neurons move together over its internal ticks: the synchronisation matrix Sij, accumulated with a decay. In this toy, 24 phase oscillators stand in for neurons. Different “inputs” couple different groups, and S takes on a different block structure.

An illustration of the idea, not the trained CIFAR model. In the real CTM, each neuron’s dynamics come from a small private network over its own history.
